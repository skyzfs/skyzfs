## Work package

M1.3e: the log's bound holds whatever the log's records are. It answers Codex's finding 4543692973 on M1.3c ("Preserve the log bound when stepping over write-zeroes records"): the checkpoint step is bounded by what it writes into the home image (8 MiB) and by the records it walks (1024). A write-zeroes record covers up to 1 MiB of the volume with one header in the log, so a step takes eight of them, 32 KiB of log, while the next write adds up to 1 MiB. With one step after every request, a log that had reached the high mark with such records grew by nearly a request with every request.

Commits: `c9c87df..c8e1956` on `zfs-topology` (one commit, 4 files). Design: docs/skyzfs-design.md §5.1 ("when the daemon checkpoints").

### 3. What a request can wait for

With the daemon's values: record alignment 4096, a step of at most 8 MiB or 1024 records, requests of at most 1 MiB (reads, writes, write-zeroes and discards alike: `max_write_zeroes_sectors` and `max_discard_sectors` are 1 MiB), a high mark of 128 MiB.

| what the log holds at the high mark | log a step takes | written into the home image by a step | steps a 1 MiB write waits for |
|---|---|---|---|
| writes | at least 7 MiB | up to 8 MiB | 1 (as before) |
| discards, alignment 4096 | 4 MiB (1024 records) | nothing | 1 |
| discards, alignment 64 | 64 KiB | nothing | 17 |
| write-zeroes of 1 MiB, alignment 4096 | 32 KiB (8 records) | 8 MiB of zeros | 33: **264 MiB of zeros and 33 syncs of the home image** |
| write-zeroes of 1 MiB, alignment 64 | 512 bytes | 8 MiB of zeros | 2049: 16 GiB of zeros |

So, to the question "can a thousand discards a step fall short of one request": at alignment 64, yes, by a factor of 16; at the daemon's alignment, no. The daemon creates volumes with 4096 only; 64 exists in the store's tests.

A log filled to the high mark with write-zeroes of 1 MiB holds 32 768 of them: 32 GiB of zeros to write in 4096 steps before the log is rid of them, and while they last a client's writes go at one 264th of the home image's pace. That is the worst case this package leaves, and I think it is too long: on a kernel (one run per image, below) the first 128 MiB written to a pool behind such a log took 131 s on 24.04 and 81 s on 26.04 (each at most 10 s too long, by the script's looks), against at most 11 s and 1 s for each of the next four files on the same runner.

**Reads wait as writes do.** The store has one worker and executes a volume's requests in the order they arrive, reads among them; that was so before, and a read between the marks waited for the one step taken after the request before it. It still does in the ordinary case of writes: one step, of up to 8 MiB, then the read. In the worst case of the table a read that arrives behind a 1 MiB write waits for that write's 33 steps like any other request, so a pool's reads behind a log of write-zeroes records go at the pace of its writes (by the same arithmetic; the kernel run had no reads in its load, so this is not measured). A read adds nothing to the log, so it causes no steps beyond the one that follows every request while the rule is working; what it waits for is what the mutation before it added.

Nothing about it is new with this package: the zeros were always written, one record's megabyte at a time. Before, the log grew instead of the request waiting.

**Proposal: do not write zeros for a write-zeroes record; deallocate the range in the home image.** The home image is a sparse file (it is made with `extend`, not `preallocate`), a hole reads as zeros, and the volume's blocks are whole blocks of the file. Then a write-zeroes record costs a step nothing to write, rewrites no write unit and so needs no preimages, and counts no bytes in a step, as a discard does: a step takes 1024 of them, 4 MiB of log at the daemon's alignment, which is more than a request adds, and the 33 steps become one. It needs: a call on the `File` trait (Linux `fallocate` with `PUNCH_HOLE | KEEP_SIZE`; the macOS and Windows equivalents exist), its model in the simulated disk (a hole not yet synced is, per block, there or not after a power cut), and a fallback to writing where the file system refuses. It also gives space back, which writing zeros takes. I have not done it: it changes what a step does to the home image and belongs in the design. The place is marked at `write_zeros`.

What is left after that proposal is alignment 64 (17 steps behind discards or holes). It is not the daemon's, and a records bound in log bytes instead of a count would remove it; I would leave it until someone wants alignment 64 in the daemon.

### 4. `make_room`

`make_room` (make the log durable and take all of it in one checkpoint) runs when an append finds the log full, and the log is full only when it has a limit on its segment files (`OpenOptions::log_segments`).

- **The daemon sets no limit**, so there the log is never full and `make_room` was unreachable before this package and is now. The log's bound in the daemon is the rule's alone, which is why this finding mattered.
- With a limit and marks, and no hold: before every request the log is below the high mark, so it never holds more than the high mark and one request. With segments of 64 MiB and a high mark of two, that is under 130 MiB, which lies in at most four segment files whatever the log's start in its first. With a limit of four or more `make_room` cannot be reached; with two or three it still can (the limit is then tighter than the marks).
- Behind a hold the rule takes nothing, the log grows to its limit, `make_room` runs, finds nothing below the hold, and the request is held. As before.

### 6. The status at the high mark

Before: the rule's first step after a load that never flushed began with the rule's own sync of 128 MiB of log, and the status said `checkpoint_step none` for as long as that took (seen on 26.04: two looks 0.3 s apart). Now the step is shown under way from before that sync. I chose "the sync counted as begun" over a third value: the sync has no other purpose than the step, a step follows it unless the sync fails the volume, and a reader of the status needs no new word.

What a reader can conclude from `checkpoint_step none` with `log_live` at or above the high mark, after this change: the worker is executing a request (between two steps), or a hold stands in front of the log (not in M1: nothing sets one in the daemon), or the volume has failed. The first passes within one request. A status that stays so over several looks, with `checkpoint_steps` not moving and requests arriving, would be a defect.

## Not done, and open questions

- **The cost behind write-zeroes records** (section 3) is stated and not removed. The proposal (deallocate the range in the home image and write nothing) is accepted in principle by the lead and is its own package, M1.3f, not this pull request. Its questions, to be settled in its description before or with the code: (1) the edges: where a range does not cover a whole write unit, zeros are written into part of a unit, so "no preimages" holds only where the volume's block, the file system's block and the write unit agree; what the store does at an edge on a 16 KiB unit; (2) durability and tearing: the log's record stays until the home image's sync returns, the simulated disk models a hole that is there or not per block after a power cut, and the power-cut tests of a step cover a step of holes; (3) what a read of a hole returns on each platform's file system, and the fallback to writing zeros where the call is refused (only Linux is built in M1); (4) a file system that refuses falls back, one that fails fails the volume; (5) the bound: such a record counts no bytes when deallocated and its bytes when the fallback writes; (6) kernel evidence: the `zerofill` prelude again, file 1 as fast as the others, and the home image's allocated size before and after.
