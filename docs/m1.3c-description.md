## Work package

M1.3c: the daemon checkpoints. The rule of docs/skyzfs-design.md §5.1, "When the daemon checkpoints": two marks on the log's live size, bounded steps on the store's worker between the requests. Commits: `skyzfs-coding..skyzfs-coding-m1.3c`, head 9cc248d (on top of M1.3b, skyzfs/skyzfs#15, at 8b809a6, which is not merged yet).

## What was done

- **A bounded step** (`skyzfs-local`, `Store::checkpoint_step`): the first durable records below the hold, at most 8 MiB of what they cover on the volume or 1024 records (`StepBound::DAEMON`), at least one (every record counts as one, a discard too; a discard counts no bytes, because a step writes nothing for it), are written into the home image, the home image is synced, and that part of the log is given up. It is one batch of the checkpoint as it was, cut shorter, in the same order (neighbours saved, home image written and synced, log's start moved and made durable, index switched). It reads the log only as far as it takes. `Store::sync_log` makes the log durable without being a request.
- **The rule** (`Store::set_marks`, `Marks`): at or above the high mark the store takes one step after every request it executes, and with no request at all, until the live size is below the low mark. If less than a step of the log is durable and more could be, it syncs the log first: the log's own sync, nobody's request, no place in the volume's order. A hold in the way stops the rule without a sync. A step that fails fails the volume. The served store applies the rule on its worker, also before its first command (a long log found by a start). In the daemon that is from the moment the device is this instance's and the worker runs, before the ready line: the start does not wait for a step, and the first request can wait for the one step under way when it arrives.
- **What the owner sees** (`Served::log_state`): the log's start, end and durable position, the steps taken, whether one is under way, and the worker's own syncs of the log (`own_syncs`, counted apart from the flushes clients ask for), published by the worker and read without asking it. It is one value behind a lock held for a copy, so it is of one moment: the start is never beyond the end, a step that has ended is never shown under way, and one that waits in its hook is. The owner is woken when a step begins and ends.
- **`serve --log-high SEGMENTS --log-low SEGMENTS`**, in every build: 2 and 1 unless given, each 1 to 1024, low below high. The store gets the marks once the device is this instance's: nothing is checkpointed before the take-over, nothing at a stop.
- **Status file version 3** (`SKYZSTA3`, 192 bytes; version 2 is still read, without the new fields): `log_live`, `checkpoint_steps`, `checkpoint_syncs` (the worker's own syncs, not among `flushes`), `checkpoint_step` (`under way` or `none`), `start_ms`, `replayed_bytes`. A step's begin and end are written at once; every other change keeps the 100 ms pace.
- **Test hooks** (`test-hooks` only): `serve --step-pause-ms N` or `--step-pause-until PATH` makes every step wait, and `--step-pause-at POINT` says where: `saved` (the blocks the step writes over are saved and durable), `written` (the home image is written and not synced; this unless given), `synced` (the home image is durable, the log starts where it did), `start-written` (inside the last part: the log's new start is written and not durable), `start-durable`. With `--step-pause-until PATH` a step that arrives at the point first makes the empty file `PATH.reached`, so a rig can kill when the step is at the point and not only under way.
- **The cost, documented** (`Marks`, "What it costs a client"): between the marks the worst case is one home-image sync per request until the low mark. The text says not to soften it without a measurement.

Toolchain: stable 1.99.1.

The tests, each written against the store's simulated disk in every environment (record alignment 64 and 4096, write unit 4 and 16 KiB, lost sectors as old content or garbage):

- the bound of a step by records and by bytes, a record beyond the bound taken alone (also a write-zeroes of 12 MiB under the daemon's bound of 8 MiB, between two small writes: the step before ends in front of it, it is a step of its own, the zeros are in the home image after a restart), nothing durable nothing done and no sync, the hold, and the cost (a step of 4 of 400 records walks under 40);
- a checkpoint done in steps, cut by power at 36 points, with a second cut in the open that follows; two planted defects of the order (the log given up before the home image is durable; neighbours not saved) are caught through the step;
- 300 writes that never flush: the log never exceeds the high mark by more than one request, steps begin only at the high mark and end below the low one, content right under the load and after a kill;
- no marks; a hold; a failing step fails the volume and the next open finds it marked;
- the served store: between requests, idle down to the low mark, a long log found at a start with no request at all, a kill, and a power cut, while the worker is inside a step, held by the hook at each of the five points in turn (the loss comes once the hook has said that the step is at the point): everything acknowledged (after a power cut: everything flushed) reads back, and the rule goes on after the restart exactly when the log that survived is still at the high mark;
- a newer record for the same block beyond a step's batch (block 5 written as 1, then as 2; the step takes the first record alone and syncs the home image). The loss is a kill with the worker held by the hook behind that sync, and a power cut after the sync of the log's new start failed; each half asserts that the log starts where it did, so that the step is applied again. After both the start reads 2, also after the step is applied again, after the steps that follow, and after another restart;
- what the worker shows of its steps never goes back while 200 000 steps begin and end beside a reader, and the log shown never starts beyond its end while 200 000 states are published that each start beyond the end of the one before;
- discards in the bound: after one write and 5000 discards a step of one record takes one record and walks at most 10; a bound of no bytes takes discards up to its records; the daemon's bound works the rest off in ceil(4899/1024) steps, none over 1024 records;
- the command line: the marks and their refusals by text, the hook's point;
- the conformance suite of `skyzfs-blk` with marks so low that steps of two records fall between its operations, at its kills and power cuts, with the store called directly and behind its worker.

## Changed in early review

The review session read `8b809a6..f425eb9` before this pull request existed. No blocking finding; six points, all answered in c6db060, and a seventh from the look at that commit, answered in 9cc248d:

1. **A defect, fixed**: discards were not counted in a step's bound, so one step took a whole log of discards (after a large trim the worker would hold every request for a walk of the whole durable log), and such a step could move the start past the end last published, which a reader between two stores could see. Every record now counts as one and a discard as zero bytes; the state shown is one value; `LogState::live` saturates. Tests `discards_count_as_records_of_a_steps_bound`, `the_log_shown_never_starts_beyond_its_end`.
2. The worker releases a held request after a step: no test covers it because it cannot happen (a request is held only behind a hold, after the log was checkpointed up to it, so the rule finds nothing to take). Kept as a safeguard and marked as one.
3. The newer-record test's kill half did not make the situation of its name (a kill keeps the written, unsynced new start). It now kills with the hook behind the home image's sync, and both halves assert where the log starts.
4. `serve.rs` states when steps begin: with the worker, before the device starts and before `ready`.
5. A refused `--log-high` or `--log-low` names the option that was given and the default it collided with.
6. The lower bound of `recovered_unacknowledged` names the record a step took before its answer was stored.
7. The tests that lose the process inside a step waited for "under way", which is set before the step begins, so the loss could land before the point the test names. The hook now makes `PATH.reached` when the step is at its point, and the tests wait for that.

## Not done, and open questions

- **Nothing of this has run on a kernel.** The acceptance there is the testing session's (M1.T7): take-over time over many kills, the log below the high mark and a step under a lasting load, kills inside steps, the suite with low marks.
- **Not measured**: the replay's rate on other hardware, a step's stall under load, and what the marks should be for a replay of about a second. The start's line and `status` now give the figures to measure with.
- In the conformance run with low marks one case (`power_cut_at_every_point_leaves_a_prefix`) can find nothing to do and is allowed to: it cuts the power between records that are not durable, and the rule's own sync has made them durable. The same suite without marks still exercises it.
- A step holds up every request for its length (design §5.1); the worker runs one step after every request while above the high mark, so a write load is slowed there: at worst one home-image sync per request until the low mark. Not measured.
- **Not tested end to end**: that the status *file* shows a step held by the hook. The store's side (the state the daemon posts) and the status writer (a begin and an end are written at once) are each tested; the pump between them needs a device. A rig check with `--step-pause-until` and `status` would close it (M1.T7).
- The status file grew from 184 to 192 bytes within version 3, which no merged build has written. If the testing session's rig already reads 184 bytes from this branch, it needs the new length.
- The suite with steps adds about a minute to `cargo test -p skyzfs-local` in a debug build.
- The marks compare the live size in bytes with N times 64 MiB, the segment size `create` gives every volume.
