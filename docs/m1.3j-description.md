## Work package

M1.3j: reads leave the store's worker.

On a kernel, a read spends most of its time in the daemon waiting for the store's one worker thread, and most of that behind a checkpoint step or the rule's own sync of the log (M1.T10: 492 to 494 µs of a read's 603 to 675 µs on the runners where the throughput criterion fails). This package gives a served store a reader thread of its own. The worker keeps every mutation, the flush, the log, the checkpoint and their order; the reader only reads. Design: `docs/skyzfs-design.md` §5.1 "Reads leave the worker", decided 2026-11-12 (zfs-topology a45338f, with the review's four points in 0629511).

Commits: `a45338f..HEAD` on `zfs-topology`, the branch `skyzfs-coding` (four work-in-progress commits, pushed as the work went, then the last). Files: `crates/skyzfs-local/src/store.rs` (the reader, the lock, the pin, the barriers, the timing), `log.rs` (reading a record from a handle of the reader's own), `format.rs` (the payload's checksum from a header), `store/tests.rs`, `tests/store_real.rs`, `crates/skyzfs-ublk/src/status.rs` and its tests (status file version 6).

### The simplification in the checked read

The proposal was: on a mismatch, wait for the worker's lock around the syncing calls and look at the failed flag; failed, answer as failed; not failed, the worker fails the volume. Both branches end in the same place: the read is answered as failed after the log is marked, and the volume has failed (by the sync that failed, or by the read that found a log it cannot read). So the reader does not wait or look: on a mismatch it hands the read to the worker, as for any read it cannot do. The worker's `fail` is idempotent. With that, the lock around the syncing calls had no user and is gone; what the worker does around a log call is to publish the durable position after the call (`Core::with_log`).

The cost in the common case is a read of the record's whole payload where the read wanted part of it, and a CRC32C over it. Counted: `time_read_unflushed_count`, `time_read_unflushed_extra_bytes`, `time_read_unflushed_mismatched`.

What changed in meaning: `time_read_*` (count, wait, call) and `time_read_wait_by_*` are now the reader's: a read's wait is laid over what the reader did (idle, which is its wake-up; the calls of reads before it; anything else), and the other twelve activities are zero for reads. `time_worker_read_ns` is zero in a served store.

The identities, exact to the nanosecond in the tests:
- a kind's fifteen `time_K_wait_by_*_ns` add up to its `time_K_wait_ns` (for reads, the reader's three do);

## Why the contract still holds

**R3 (content and read).** A read received after an operation was acknowledged observes it: the worker enters a mutation into the index before it acknowledges it, and the reader looks up after the read was submitted, so after the acknowledgement. A read that overlaps an operation in flight sees each block before or after it; here it sees the whole operation or none of it, because a read's range is looked up under one hold of the lock and a mutation's range entered under one. What it reads is what the index named: a log record (read from the reader's own handle; a slot is not reused before the second barrier), zeros, or the home image (not written before the first barrier for a reader that looked up before it). The model's rule that a write a read has shown stays shown holds with one reader, which looks up in the order it was handed reads; the index only moves forward for a block.

**R8 (order).** Mutations are executed by the worker alone, in the order of submission, as before; the reader executes no mutation. A read is not ordered against a mutation in flight, which R8 does not ask. What changed on purpose: a read behind a held write (the log full behind a hold) is answered and no longer held with it.

**R9 (daemon loss).** Nothing the reader does is written, so what a lost daemon leaves is what the worker left, as before. An unanswered read is reissued by the client like any request in flight. A read the reader could not do is answered only after the worker has marked the log; if the daemon is lost in between, no failed read was delivered and the next start finds no mark (`a_failed_read_is_not_answered_before_the_log_is_marked`).

**R10 (power loss).** The writes to the disk and their order are the worker's, unchanged: see the next section. A read served from a record that was not durable shows what a flush has not promised yet, as before (a client is served its own acknowledged write before any sync); the checked read makes sure the bytes served are the ones written, also beside a sync that fails.

**R11 (failed volume).** From the failure every later request fails: the worker sets the shared failed flag when it fails the volume, before it answers anything, and the reader checks it at every read's begin. A read the reader cannot do is answered as failed only after the log is marked. A read in flight at the failure completes either way: a read of a record not yet durable is served only if its payload matches its checksum, so it is never served with what a failed sync left in the cache (`a_read_of_an_unflushed_record_is_not_answered_from_a_failed_log`, which is how the conformance suite found the case on the first run with the reader).

## Nothing written to disk or its order changes, and how I know

- The reader writes nothing: it calls only `read_at` on its handles. The worker's I/O calls are the same calls in the same order: the changes on the worker are the two barriers (waits, no I/O), the index under a lock, the durable position published after a log call, and the answer to a read the reader could not do (the volume's failure and its mark, which a failed read caused on the worker before).
- The tests that would see a change of what is written or of its order pass unchanged: every cut test (`a_checkpoint_in_steps_can_be_cut_anywhere`, `steps_of_holes_and_writes_can_be_cut_anywhere`, `a_write_inside_a_zeroed_range_can_be_cut_anywhere`, `a_volume_whose_unit_is_at_least_the_block_survives_cuts_anywhere`), the kill tests, the conformance suite's power-cut and kill cases through the store called directly and through the served store, with and without marks, and every mutant test of the order inside a step (`RECLAIM_BEFORE_HOME_SYNC`, `INDEX_SWITCH_EARLY`, `CHECKPOINT_UNSYNCED` and the rest).
- Every existing mutant is still caught: all `caught` tests pass. None lost its meaning: `READ_HOME_ONLY`, `READ_ERROR_SERVED` and `SERVE_AFTER_FAILURE` act in the reader as on the worker.

## Not done, and what I am unsure of

- **One reader, and no parameter.** The lead's package says the number is a parameter that stays at one. I did not add the parameter: with one reader nothing can test it, and more readers need the model's rule "a write a read has shown stays shown" shown with reads that overlap each other in time first (the reviewer sees no way for several readers to break it, given row 3; nothing here shows it). Adding the parameter is a channel shared by several reader threads and a count; I would do it with that test.
- **Review point 2 in a served store.** With one reader the pins in a served store never overlap, so the barrier's fairness is shown on the pin itself and not through the store.
- **The torn block** behind the first barrier is assumed of the kernel, not shown (above).
- **A reader's lookup and the durable position** are read one after the other, not under one lock: a record found not durable may have become durable meanwhile, which only means one more checked read than needed. The other way round cannot happen: the durable position is published only after the sync that made it so has returned.
- **The cost of the checked reads** on a kernel is unknown until a benchmark run counts them (`time_read_unflushed_*`).
- **The barriers' cost** on a kernel likewise (`time_barrier_*`, `time_worker_step_barrier_ns`).
- **Two hand-overs** per read remain (to the reader and back), as decided.
