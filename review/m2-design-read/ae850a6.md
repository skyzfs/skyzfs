# The amended §5.2 (zfs-topology ae850a6), checked against the seven findings

Six of the seven are right as written. Finding 1's rule still has a gap. It opened when finding 3 moved replacement to the newer cut's flush completion: for the length of that flush a third cut exists, and the text does not say how the two rules apply to it.

## The window

- X's instant is the barrier, where its flush runs `begin_sync`. The sync is started on the file's helper (crates/skyzfs-local/src/inplace.rs:1194-1213 at 744db83).
- From then until the sync ends, `accept` (:1281) runs later writes on the threads.
- So between X's instant and X's flush completing, the store applies writes while W still waits and X does not yet replace it.

## (a) At the bound, which cut takes the abandoned cut's map and copies (changes the text; severe if read the other way)

**The text.** At the bound "its map goes to the newest cut", and "those copies become the newest cut's".

**The trouble.** In the window the newest cut is X, whose flush has not completed. If the map and the copies go to X and X's flush then fails, the volume has failed. W is then uploaded by the instance that saw the failure (the lead's call), and W has neither.

**A case.**
- The last upload was at group 100. U is at 140, W at 150, and X at 151, with X's flush under way. The bound is reached in that flush.
- U's map and its copies go to X.
- X's copy of slot 23, which held group 119's uberblock, is freed: slot 23 is in X's own map, since group 151 wrote it.
- X's flush fails.
- W's upload puts the changes from 140 to 150 onto the image at 100. Every block written from 101 to 140 and not written again before 150 keeps its content from 100. Slot 23 reads group 151.
- The result is a corrupt recovery point, at the one moment it is most needed.

**Fix.** At the bound the abandoned cut's map, and the copies the next cut needs, go to the cut that waits: the newest cut whose flush has completed. A cut whose flush is under way gets them when it replaces that one. Only with no cut waiting do they go to the cut in flight; if its flush then fails, no cut is uploadable anyway.

## (b) At replacement, not every copy of the replaced cut may be freed (changes the text; low)

**The text.** "The copies made for the replaced cut are freed."

**What I said before, and where it stops.** In finding 1 I said replacement is right as written, because every copy of W holds a block that X's own interval rewrote. That holds for replacement at X's instant. It does not hold for replacement at X's flush completion.

**The case it misses.** A write in the window to a block in W's map but not in X's own map makes one copy, and that copy is X's content as well. Freeing it at replacement leaves X holding a write received after X's flush.

**The harm.**
- As I read OpenZFS, nothing ZFS sends in that window depends on the flush. The odd labels and the next group wait for it to complete. ZIL and multihost writes in the window are ones a power cut may keep, and `zil_claim_write` ends the log at a record whose block is not there.
- So the pool still imports.
- But the cut is no longer the volume at its instant, which M2.2's model and M2.T1's reference check.

**Fix.** At replacement, the same rule as at the bound: W's copies of blocks outside X's own map are X's. The bold "a copy is freed only when no held cut needs it" says it, but the replacement bullet contradicts it as worded.

## For M2.2's tests

- **Two more mutants:**
  - an abandoned map given to a cut whose flush is under way;
  - the replaced cut's copies all freed.
- **What the property test then has to drive:**
  - a write between a cut's instant and its flush's completion, to a block outside that cut's own map;
  - the bound reached in that window, followed by the flush failing.
